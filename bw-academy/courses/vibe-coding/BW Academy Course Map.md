# BW Academy Course Map: Vibe Coding & App Development
**Program outcome:** Learners go from "I have an idea" to building and launching real software that serves real people, and they understand how it works well enough to direct AI tools instead of hoping the tools get it right.
**Pillars (in order):** 1. Intro to Vibe Coding (Vibe Coding I) · 2. Build Your First App (Vibe Coding I) · 3. Game Design Studio (Vibe Coding II) · 4. Launch Lab: Ship a Real Product (Vibe Coding II)
**Audience:** Students about 12–18, beginners · **Format:** 4 courses × 18 sessions, 3 per week, about 20–30 min each
**Status:** All four courses are planned and awaiting approval. No lessons have been built yet.

---

## Pillar territories
- **1 · Intro to Vibe Coding:** owns what vibe coding is, how the web works (HTML, CSS, JavaScript), prompting, reading AI code, design basics and accessibility, AI's limits and responsibility, why we create, and simple publishing. · Home lesson for: prompting, AI's limits, why we create.
- **2 · Build Your First App:** owns problem to plan, specs and breaking work into steps, data, logic, saving data, testing, the debugging method, version control and resilience. · Home lesson for: debugging, version control.
- **3 · Game Design Studio:** owns fun and mechanics, the game loop, movement, collisions, scoring, levels, art and sound, game feel, team roles, merging work, team conflict and playtesting. · Home lesson for: teamwork, playtesting.
- **4 · Launch Lab:** owns client discovery and interviewing, MVP scope, user journeys, tool and cost choices, accounts, privacy, security, real launch, onboarding, metrics, feedback and iteration, maintenance and handoff, presenting, and calling. · Home lesson for: interviewing, user feedback, serving others with technology.

## Pillar 1: Intro to Vibe Coding (status: planned)
**Course outcome:** By the end of 6 weeks, learners will be able to build and publish a one-page website by describing their ideas to an AI tool, and explain how the HTML, CSS and JavaScript behind it work.
**Competencies:** Practical creativity, critical thinking · **Final project:** a one-page website for a family member, church or local business.

### Week 1: Welcome to Vibe Coding, what it is and why it's possible now
- **W1-S01 You Can Build That:** Vibe coding means describing what you want in plain words while AI writes the code, and you're the director. | anchor: Genesis 2:19 | challenge: List three things you wish existed.
- **W1-S02 What's Really Behind a Website:** Every site is made of HTML (structure), CSS (style) and JavaScript (behaviour). | anchor: Proverbs 24:3 | challenge: Inspect a favourite site and find all three.
- **W1-S03 Your First Prompt, Your First Page:** Build a first page with AI. The first draft is a start, not the finish. | anchor: Zechariah 4:10 | challenge: Make an "about me" page and change one thing yourself.

### Week 2: Speaking AI's Language, the craft of prompting (home lesson for prompting)
- **W2-S04 Say What You Mean:** Good prompts state the goal, the details and an example. | anchor: Habakkuk 2:2 | challenge: Rewrite one vague prompt three ways and compare the results.
- **W2-S05 One Step at a Time:** Small, focused prompts beat one giant prompt. | anchor: Luke 16:10 | challenge: Build one section of a page using five small prompts.
- **W2-S06 Talking Back to the AI:** Give the AI feedback and ask it to explain its code, so you learn as you build. | anchor: Proverbs 18:15 | challenge: Have the AI explain three lines of your page in plain words.

### Week 3: Reading the Code, understanding what the AI wrote
- **W3-S07 HTML: The Skeleton:** Tags give a page its structure: headings, paragraphs, images and links. | anchor: Psalm 139:14 | challenge: Add a list, an image and a link by editing the code yourself.
- **W3-S08 CSS: Giving It Style:** Colours, fonts and spacing are rules you can read and change by hand. | anchor: Ecclesiastes 3:11 | challenge: Choose a 3-colour palette and apply it.
- **W3-S09 JavaScript: Making It Move:** Events make a page respond to people, for example a button that does something. | anchor: Proverbs 14:23 | challenge: Add a button that changes something on the page.

### Week 4: Design That Works for People
- **W4-S10 Layout Like a Pro:** Visual hierarchy, sections, white space and phone-friendly layouts. | anchor: 1 Corinthians 14:40 | challenge: Sketch the layout on paper before prompting.
- **W4-S11 Design for Everyone:** Accessibility: colour contrast, alt text and readable fonts, so no one is shut out. | anchor: Leviticus 19:14 | challenge: Add alt text to every image and check your contrast.
- **W4-S12 Words That Work:** Clear headlines and calls to action make a site useful. | anchor: Colossians 4:6 | challenge: Rewrite your headline for a real visitor.

### Week 5: Thinking Critically About AI (home lesson for AI's limits and for why we create)
- **W5-S13 When AI Gets It Wrong:** AI can be confidently wrong, so test what it gives you. | anchor: 1 Thessalonians 5:21 | challenge: Catch and fix one AI mistake.
- **W5-S14 You're the Responsible One:** You own what you publish: accuracy, honesty, and permission to use images and content. | anchor: Galatians 6:5 | challenge: Check the source and licence of every image on your page.
- **W5-S15 Why We Create:** Creativity and skill are gifts from the Creator, meant to be used well. | anchor: Exodus 31:3–5 | challenge: Write a one-sentence purpose for your site.

### Week 6: Build & Publish (final project)
- **W6-S16 Plan the Real Site:** Gather the content (words, photos, details) and map out the page before building. | anchor: Proverbs 20:5 | challenge: Collect everything you need from the person you're building for.
- **W6-S17 Build It Start to Finish:** Use the whole workflow to build the complete site: plan, prompt, read, refine. | anchor: Nehemiah 4:6 | challenge: Finish a full draft of the site.
- **W6-S18 Go Live!:** Publish the site on free hosting, share the link and celebrate. | anchor: Matthew 5:14–16 | challenge: Send the live link to the person you built it for.

## Pillar 2: Build Your First App (status: planned)
**Course outcome:** By the end of 6 weeks, learners will be able to plan, prompt, test, debug and ship a working web app that solves a real problem at home.
**Competencies:** Critical thinking, resilience · **Final project:** an app that solves one real problem at home, such as a chore chart or budget tracker.

### Week 1: Find the Problem
- **W1-S01 Apps Solve Problems:** Unlike a website, an app responds to people and remembers things for them. | anchor: Philippians 2:4 | challenge: List five annoyances at home.
- **W1-S02 Pick a Problem Worth Solving:** Choose one small, real problem and name exactly who it's for. | anchor: Nehemiah 2:17 | challenge: Ask a family member what slows them down every week.
- **W1-S03 Sketch Before You Build:** A paper sketch of each screen and how users move between them saves hours. | anchor: Luke 14:28 | challenge: Draw three screens on paper.

### Week 2: Plan It Out
- **W2-S04 Write the Spec:** A one-page plan (features, must-haves, nice-to-haves) becomes your master prompt. | anchor: Proverbs 21:5 | challenge: Write your spec.
- **W2-S05 Break It Into Pieces:** Split the app into small, buildable steps in a sensible order. | anchor: Exodus 18:21–22 | challenge: List eight build steps in order.
- **W2-S06 Data: What Your App Remembers:** Variables, lists and records explained in plain terms. | anchor: Luke 2:19 | challenge: Write out every piece of data your app needs.

### Week 3: Build It
- **W3-S07 Build the First Slice:** Get one feature working from start to finish before adding more. | anchor: Ecclesiastes 11:4 | challenge: Get one feature fully working.
- **W3-S08 Making Decisions in Code:** If/else logic lets your app respond to what the user does. | anchor: Joshua 24:15 | challenge: Add one rule, such as "if the chore is done, show a check mark".
- **W3-S09 Remember Me: Saving Data:** Saving to the browser means the app still remembers after a refresh. | anchor: Joshua 4:6–7 | challenge: Make your data survive a page refresh.

### Week 4: Break It (home lesson for the debugging method)
- **W4-S10 Test Like a Troublemaker:** Try to break your own app on purpose with odd inputs and edge cases. | anchor: Lamentations 3:40 | challenge: Find ten ways to break your app.
- **W4-S11 The Debugging Method:** Reproduce the bug, narrow it down, read the error, guess the cause, then change one thing at a time. | anchor: Proverbs 25:2 | challenge: Start a bug journal.
- **W4-S12 Debugging with AI as Your Partner:** Give the AI the error, the context and what you've already tried, instead of looping "fix it" over and over. | anchor: Proverbs 15:22 | challenge: Fix a bug using a well-written AI bug report.

### Week 5: Fix It & Keep Going (home lesson for version control and resilience)
- **W5-S13 Save Points: Version Control:** Commits are save points that let you undo mistakes without fear. | anchor: Lamentations 3:22–23 | challenge: Make five meaningful save points.
- **W5-S14 When It Won't Work:** Frustration is part of building. Take a break, ask for help, and keep going. | anchor: Galatians 6:9 | challenge: Write your personal "when I'm stuck" plan.
- **W5-S15 Make It Easy to Use:** Clear buttons, labels and messages make an app easy to understand. | anchor: 1 Corinthians 14:9 | challenge: Rewrite every button and message in plain words.

### Week 6: Ship It
- **W6-S16 Finish the Must-Haves:** Finishing beats adding: cut the nice-to-haves and complete the core. | anchor: 2 Timothy 4:7 | challenge: Check off every must-have.
- **W6-S17 Ship It Home:** Put the app on the family's devices and have them really use it. | anchor: James 1:22 | challenge: Have someone use your app every day for a week.
- **W6-S18 Look Back, Level Up:** Reflect on what you learned and plan version two. | anchor: Philippians 3:13–14 | challenge: Write a version 2 list and demo the app.

## Pillar 3: Game Design Studio (status: planned)
**Course outcome:** By the end of 6 weeks, learners will be able to design, build, playtest and improve a browser game as part of a team.
**Competencies:** Practical creativity, teamwork · **Final project:** a playable game, tested by three people, with the biggest issue fixed.

### Week 1: What Makes Games Fun
- **W1-S01 Welcome to the Studio:** Games are designed experiences, and a studio has roles. | anchor: Psalm 104:26 | challenge: Play a simple game and write down what makes it fun.
- **W1-S02 Rules, Goals, and Challenge:** Every game has rules, a goal and obstacles, kept in balance. | anchor: 2 Timothy 2:5 | challenge: Invent a paper game with three rules.
- **W1-S03 Pitch Your Game:** A one-page concept and a 60-second pitch, then the team picks its project. | anchor: Proverbs 27:17 | challenge: Pitch your idea in 60 seconds.

### Week 2: The Engine Room
- **W2-S04 The Game Loop:** Update, draw, repeat: the heartbeat of every game. | anchor: Ecclesiastes 1:5–7 | challenge: Get a shape moving on screen.
- **W2-S05 Controls and Movement:** Keyboard input and smooth movement make the player feel in control. | anchor: Psalm 18:33 | challenge: Tune the speed until movement feels right.
- **W2-S06 Collisions:** Detecting when things touch, such as walls, enemies and pickups. | anchor: Proverbs 22:3 | challenge: Add obstacles that end the round.

### Week 3: Score, Win, Level Up
- **W3-S07 Keeping Score:** Score, lives, timers and the on-screen display reward progress. | anchor: Matthew 25:21 | challenge: Add a score and a high score.
- **W3-S08 Levels and Difficulty:** A good difficulty curve is hard enough to stretch players but never impossible. | anchor: 1 Corinthians 10:13 | challenge: Design three levels that get harder.
- **W3-S09 Win, Lose, Try Again:** Game states (start, play, game over, restart) keep players coming back. | anchor: Micah 7:8 | challenge: Add a game-over screen and a restart.

### Week 4: Art, Sound, and Feel
- **W4-S10 Look and Style:** A consistent art style, and using AI-made or borrowed art honestly. | anchor: Psalm 19:1 | challenge: Create a matching set of game art.
- **W4-S11 Sound and Juice:** Sound effects, music and visual feedback bring a game to life. | anchor: Psalm 33:3 | challenge: Add three sound effects.
- **W4-S12 Game Feel:** Polish the small moments so the game feels responsive and satisfying. | anchor: Ecclesiastes 9:10 | challenge: Improve one moment of feel.

### Week 5: Build as a Team (home lesson for teamwork)
- **W5-S13 Roles in the Studio:** Divide the work so each person clearly owns part of it. | anchor: 1 Corinthians 12:12–20 | challenge: Make a team role chart.
- **W5-S14 Merging Our Work:** Combine teammates' code safely, building on Course 2's save points. | anchor: Amos 3:3 | challenge: Merge a teammate's feature into the game.
- **W5-S15 Creative Differences:** Disagree well and give feedback kindly. | anchor: Proverbs 15:1 | challenge: Run a 10-minute team retro (what went well, what was hard).

### Week 6: Playtest & Polish (home lesson for playtesting)
- **W6-S16 Run a Playtest:** Watch quietly, take notes and don't explain: the game has to speak for itself. | anchor: James 1:19 | challenge: Have three people playtest your game.
- **W6-S17 Fix the Biggest Issue:** Sort the feedback, fix the one thing that matters most, and test again. | anchor: Proverbs 12:1 | challenge: Fix the top issue and retest.
- **W6-S18 Release Day:** Share the game, give credit to everyone who helped, and celebrate. | anchor: Psalm 126:3 | challenge: Host a team arcade night.

## Pillar 4: Launch Lab: Ship a Real Product (status: planned)
**Course outcome:** By the end of 6 weeks, learners will be able to discover a real need with a ministry or nonprofit, then build, launch and improve a tool for them and present it.
**Competencies:** Entrepreneurship, serving others · **Final project:** a tool for a ministry or nonprofit, presented to them.

### Week 1: Find a Real Need (home lesson for serving others and interviewing)
- **W1-S01 From Project to Product:** A product has real users who depend on it, and building one is a way to serve. | anchor: Mark 10:45 | challenge: List three ministries or nonprofits you could serve.
- **W1-S02 Meet Your Client:** Interview well: listen for the real problem underneath the request. | anchor: Proverbs 18:13 | challenge: Hold an interview with your client.
- **W1-S03 Define the Problem:** A clear problem statement and a measure of success, confirmed by the client. | anchor: Proverbs 19:2 | challenge: Get your client to confirm your problem statement.

### Week 2: Plan the Product
- **W2-S04 Version One:** A minimum viable product means choosing the few things that matter and saying no to the rest. | anchor: Luke 10:41–42 | challenge: Cut your feature list to three core features.
- **W2-S05 User Journeys:** Map how different real people will move through the tool. | anchor: 1 Corinthians 9:22 | challenge: Write two user profiles and their journeys.
- **W2-S06 Choosing Your Tools:** Pick hosting and data storage the client can afford and keep running. | anchor: 1 Corinthians 4:2 | challenge: Make a tools-and-costs sheet for your client.

### Week 3: Build It Right
- **W3-S07 Accounts and Logins:** Use trusted login services rather than building your own. | anchor: Nehemiah 7:3 | challenge: Add a login using a trusted service.
- **W3-S08 Protecting People's Data:** Collect only what you need, ask permission and keep it safe. | anchor: Proverbs 11:13 | challenge: Write a plain-language privacy note.
- **W3-S09 Security Basics:** Hide secret keys, check user input, and review AI-written code for risks (building on Course 1, lesson 14). | anchor: Matthew 10:16 | challenge: Go through a security checklist.

### Week 4: Launch
- **W4-S10 Real Launch:** A live web address, a proper deployment, and keeping test and live versions separate (building on Course 1's simple publishing). | anchor: Proverbs 16:3 | challenge: Put your tool online at a live address.
- **W4-S11 Onboarding Your First Users:** First impressions, instructions, and training the people who will use the tool. | anchor: 2 Timothy 2:2 | challenge: Write a one-page user guide.
- **W4-S12 Measure What Matters:** Decide what success looks like and track it. | anchor: Proverbs 27:23 | challenge: Set up a feedback form and one success measure.

### Week 5: Listen & Improve (home lesson for user feedback)
- **W5-S13 Feedback Is a Gift:** Collect feedback and receive it without getting defensive. | anchor: Proverbs 27:6 | challenge: Collect five pieces of user feedback.
- **W5-S14 Ship, Learn, Repeat:** Prioritise improvements and release updates. | anchor: Jeremiah 18:3–4 | challenge: Release one improvement.
- **W5-S15 Keeping It Running:** Maintenance and handoff notes: who looks after the tool once you're done. | anchor: 1 Corinthians 3:6 | challenge: Write a handoff document.

### Week 6: Present & Multiply
- **W6-S16 Tell the Story:** Tell the story of the product in a clear demo: the problem, the journey and the impact. | anchor: Acts 14:27 | challenge: Draft a 5-minute demo.
- **W6-S17 Present to Your Client:** Present with confidence, answer questions, and hand over the tool. | anchor: Colossians 3:23 | challenge: Present to your ministry or nonprofit.
- **W6-S18 Builders Who Serve:** Your calling and future in technology: keep building things that serve. | anchor: 1 Peter 4:10 | challenge: Write your builder's mission statement and your next idea.
